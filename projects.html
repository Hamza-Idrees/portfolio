01 — Multi-tenant SaaS

Obenan

Multi-tenant location and reputation management.

I contributed backend APIs and workflows for reviews, reporting, location synchronization, and AI-assisted content on a platform serving 400+ businesses across 1,000+ locations in 35+ countries during my work.

What is Obenan?

Obenan is a digital-presence platform for businesses with physical locations, including restaurants, cafés, and hospitality brands. It helps them keep business information consistent online, manage their reputation, and make their locations easier to discover through local search and AI assistants.

Who uses it and why?

Business owners and teams managing multiple locations need to maintain information, publish updates, and respond to customer feedback across different platforms. Obenan brings these activities into connected workflows so each location’s online presence is easier to manage.

How the product works

  1. Business information provides the context for how a location is represented online.
  2. Content workflows prepare local updates, while review workflows draft replies and answers from approved business facts.
  3. Teams can approve replies or configure automatic responses. The platform also supports visibility in AI-driven discovery.

Product overview: Obenan’s official website ↗

The engineering challenge

Business-location data and customer reviews arrive from multiple external platforms. The backend needs to bring those sources together while supporting reporting, access control, and content workflows for many businesses.

My contribution

I developed and optimized multi-tenant REST APIs, maintained relational and document-based data workflows, and added Redis caching to frequently accessed endpoints. Background jobs coordinate ingestion, reporting, and platform synchronization without tying those operations to a single API request.

Node.js / Express / PostgreSQL / MongoDB / Redis / AWS

What I built

  • Dual-database architecture with 100+ Sequelize models and Redis caching.
  • Optimized batching and pagination to ingest 40,000+ reviews within Lambda’s 15-minute limit.
  • 10+ BullMQ queues supporting ingestion, analysis, reports, email, and synchronization.
  • Batched OpenAI and Claude processing for 100+ posts in under 30 seconds.

02 — Real-time systems

Reddr

Backend infrastructure for personal safety.

I built authenticated REST and WebSocket interfaces, location-aware backend workflows, and push-notification processing, and deployed the backend on AWS.

What is Reddr?

Reddr is a personal-safety app for sending an SOS alert to people a user trusts. An alert shares emergency information and the location available when SOS is triggered with accepted emergency contacts and members of the user’s groups.

Who uses it and why?

The app organizes connections around loved ones and groups, such as family, neighbours, or colleagues. It gives users a direct way to notify their emergency circle when they need help, alongside—not as a replacement for—emergency services.

The core user journey

  1. The user adds trusted contacts and organizes their groups.
  2. Pressing and holding SOS starts an emergency alert with available location information.
  3. The emergency circle is notified, and the user can resolve the emergency when it is over.

The public website describes continuous live sharing and the nearby helper network as planned features. My contribution below describes the backend interfaces and workflows I built, rather than a claim that every capability is publicly available.

Product overview: Reddr’s official website ↗

The engineering challenge

A mobile panic event needs to reach backend emergency workflows while location data continues to change. The application also needs authenticated communication between the mobile client, real-time map, and notification services.

My contribution

I designed the backend around NestJS and Prisma, using PostgreSQL/PostGIS for location-aware data. I implemented authenticated REST and WebSocket interfaces for live location updates and emergency events, then connected BullMQ processing with Socket.IO and Firebase push notifications.

NestJS / Prisma / PostGIS / Socket.IO / BullMQ / Docker

What I built

  • PostgreSQL/PostGIS for location-aware data and Redis/BullMQ for background processing.
  • Socket.IO location updates and Firebase Cloud Messaging notifications.
  • Docker deployment on EC2 with RDS, S3, ECR, CodePipeline, CodeBuild, SSM, and Prisma migrations.

03 — Booking automation

The Nanny Line

Nanny booking, pricing, and scheduling automation.

Cross-channel booking automation covering request capture, deterministic pricing, nanny matching, availability, and calendar scheduling.

What is The Nanny Line?

An AI-powered booking system that coordinates nanny requests received through WhatsApp and email. It connects the conversation with the operational steps needed to turn a request into a confirmed and scheduled booking.

Who does it support?

The workflow connects people requesting childcare, nannies responding to availability checks, and the team coordinating bookings. It tracks the conversation and booking state so a request can progress across multiple communication channels without being duplicated.

From request to booking

  1. Capture the request and maintain structured conversation-level state.
  2. Calculate pricing using location, holiday, overnight, travel, VAT, and commission rules.
  3. Match location and language requirements, broadcast availability requests, and collect responses.
  4. Coordinate nanny selection, reconfirm the booking 24 hours ahead, and schedule it in Google Calendar.

The engineering challenge

Booking a nanny involves more than collecting a date. Requests arrive through different channels, pricing depends on several business rules, and a booking needs matching, availability checks, selection, and reconfirmation before scheduling.

My contribution

I automated the workflow across WhatsApp and email with conversation-level state in Google Sheets. A deterministic pricing engine handles location rates, holidays, overnight work, travel compensation, VAT, and commission. The workflow coordinates nanny responses and carries the selected booking through to Google Calendar.

n8n / OpenAI / Claude / WhatsApp / Google APIs

What I built

  • Conversation-level state and deduplication across WhatsApp and email.
  • Pricing rules for location, holidays, overnight work, travel, VAT, and agency commission.
  • Location and language matching, availability broadcasts, selection, and 24-hour reconfirmation.
  • Google Calendar scheduling with safeguards for API limits and workflow loops.

04 — Practice automation

Rosa

An AI receptionist for Solar Chiropractic.

Appointment booking, rescheduling, cancellation, and operational follow-ups across WhatsApp and email, connected to PracticeHub.

What is Rosa?

Rosa is an AI receptionist and practice-automation system for Solar Chiropractic. It connects appointment conversations on WhatsApp and email with PracticeHub, the practice system used by the workflow.

Who does it support?

Patients can request appointment changes through familiar communication channels, while practice staff receive cases that require human input. The system also handles operational follow-ups such as reminders, recalls, payment-related workflows, and no-show follow-ups.

How a request is handled

  1. Receive an appointment-related conversation through WhatsApp or email.
  2. Apply booking rules for same-day requests, recurring reviews, reactivation, and family or on-behalf bookings.
  3. Coordinate booking, rescheduling, or cancellation through PracticeHub, using contact-level privacy controls.
  4. Send confirmations and follow-ups, retry failed operations, or escalate exceptions to practice staff.

The engineering challenge

A practice receptionist must handle appointment conversations while respecting booking restrictions and patient context. Routine requests should be automated, while exceptions need a clear path to practice staff.

My contribution

I built a production AI receptionist connected to PracticeHub, WhatsApp, and email. Deterministic rules govern same-day restrictions, recurring reviews, reactivation, and family bookings. A Pinecone retrieval layer supports conversations, with contact-level privacy controls and staff escalation when human input is required.

n8n / PracticeHub / Pinecone / Claude / WhatsApp

What I built

  • Deterministic booking rules for same-day restrictions, reviews, reactivation, and family bookings.
  • Pinecone-based retrieval with contact-level privacy controls.
  • Confirmations, reminders, recalls, payments, no-show workflows, and practice reports.
  • Retry handling and escalation to staff when human input is needed.

05 — Retrieval-augmented generation

Lumen

Natural-language search over clinical records.

A clinical search assistant with intent extraction, filtered vector retrieval, and streamed answers grounded in patient records.

What is Lumen?

Lumen is a clinical search and question-answering assistant. It lets users ask questions about patient data in natural language and receive answers based on retrieved records, rather than relying on a language model’s general knowledge.

What problem does it solve?

A useful patient-data search often combines several constraints: a condition, an age range, a doctor, a date, or billing information. Lumen converts those constraints into structured filters and combines them with semantic retrieval to find relevant records.

From question to answer

  1. Extract the user’s intent and relevant metadata filters with GPT-4o-mini.
  2. Create an embedding and search Qdrant using the extracted constraints.
  3. Apply similarity thresholds and fallback handling when retrieval confidence is low.
  4. Use the retrieved records as context and stream the response through server-sent events.

The engineering challenge

Natural-language questions about patient records need more than semantic similarity. Clinical, demographic, date, and billing constraints must narrow the search, and responses need to stay grounded in retrieved records.

My contribution

I built the pipeline from intent extraction and embeddings through Qdrant search and SSE streaming. The query parser supports more than 20 metadata filters, while similarity thresholds and fallback handling address low-confidence results. The system supports bulk ingestion of 5,000 records in under 60 seconds.

Python / FastAPI / Qdrant / OpenAI / SSE / Next.js

What I built

  • GPT-4o-mini intent extraction, OpenAI embeddings, Qdrant search, and SSE streaming.
  • A 20+ filter parser covering conditions, age, doctors, dates, billing, and blood type.
  • Similarity thresholds and fallback handling for low-confidence queries.
  • Bulk ingestion of 5,000 records in under 60 seconds.
